# Using mekaweb

This guide covers signing in and the interface's behavior in detail. See [Installing mekaweb](install.md) for setup.

## Signing in

Open mekaweb at its address and sign in with your username and password, or with **Use a passkey**. Where the browser offers it, the username field suggests your passkeys as you focus it. A sign-in lasts 30 days after its last use, up to a year. A wrong password is refused after as long as a right one takes to check. Repeated tries from one network, or password guesses at the account from everywhere, wait before trying again; a passkey is never held up by someone else guessing the password.

Your name at the foot of the navigation opens these settings, and the button beside it signs out. **Settings** holds the account:

- **Passkeys:** **Add a passkey** makes one on this device or a security key, named as you choose. Passkeys need mekaweb to be reached by name over HTTPS, or on `localhost`; a passkey made at one hostname works only there. Removing one stops it signing in to mekaweb; the authenticator keeps its copy until you delete it there.
- **Password:** at least 10 characters, and not the username. Changing it takes the current one, and signs out every other device.
- **Devices:** every browser signed in, with its browser and system, when it was last seen, and its address; point at one for when and how it signed in. Rename one, or sign it out; signing a device out ends what it has open, its conversations' live updates and its terminals' connections, and its push notifications. **Sign out other devices** signs out every device but this one.
- **Sign-in activity:** recent sign-ins, confirmations and password changes, refused ones included, with their address and device.

Adding or removing a passkey needs you to have confirmed who you are in the last 10 minutes: mekaweb asks for a passkey, or the password if you have none. Signing in counts.

Signing out forgets what this browser kept of the account: unsent drafts, which conversations it showed, and uploads under way. If mekaweb cannot be reached to end the sign-in, the device stays signed in and says so.

## Features

Start a conversation by describing a task and sending it. The folder button below the composer sets the working directory: enter an absolute path on the meka server, or choose one of your recent directories. meka refuses a folder that is not there; **Make this folder** makes it, with any folders above it. The button beside it puts the session on a project. Profile and permission mode sit beside Send; approval mode and delivery options are under **Settings**. A session is created only when you send. **New session** sets this screen up like the session you are in; pressed again, or with no session open, it uses the server's defaults. Either way, an unsent message and its images stay. If the first message fails after creation, retry in that session.

- **Messages:** Enter sends; Shift+Enter adds a newline. The composer starts at two lines and grows with longer drafts, up to three quarters of the conversation's height. Drag its top grip, or focus it and use arrow keys, to set a height yourself; it then stays fixed until the next send. Its height resets after a successful send. When idle, a message starts a turn, which keeps running if you close the tab. While the agent works, messages use Steer by default; choose Queue or Interrupt in the composer's Settings. When a turn is running and the input is empty, Send becomes a red Stop button for that turn. A scheduled job's fire and the report of a finished background task show as notes from meka, within the agent's turn when a report reaches it mid-turn; expand one to read what the agent was given.
- **Images:** Attach images with the image button, by pasting them into the message input, or by dropping them anywhere on the conversation. A paste that also holds text, as from a spreadsheet, pastes the text. Images are sent as they are up to meka's 3.75 MB limit, in PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP, or a raster format meka converts, such as BMP or TIFF. A larger image is shrunk in the browser until it fits, keeping its format where it can (a PNG becomes a JPEG only when it would otherwise shrink below 1024 pixels), and its name says so; a shrunk image loses its metadata, such as where a photo was taken. To give the agent an original, upload it in the session's Files and mention it. Other files, including SVG, HEIC, and PDF, are refused.
- **Conversation appearance:** Set **Conversation font size** and **Conversation max width** under **Settings → Appearance**. Both are kept in this browser, as is the rest of **Appearance**, since screens differ. Width applies to the chat and composer, with a full-width option; font size applies to messages, their contents, and the text you type. Other pages and control text retain their sizes.
- **Conversation position:** The conversation is centered on the page by default, so opening or closing panels does not move it. Set **Center conversation on** to **Conversation area** to center it between the panels instead. **Conversation offset** moves it by a number of pixels, negative to the left. For vertical browser tabs or a tab sidebar, about half the sidebar's width re-centers it on the window. The conversation always stays within the conversation area at its full width.
- **Reading options:** Open **Aa** in the session or new conversation header to enter a font size or max width in pixels, then press **Enter** to apply. The **− / +** buttons and arrow keys apply 1 px font or 100 px width steps immediately. Leave width blank for full width; width also applies to the composer. Under **Position**, choose **Page** or **Area** to center on, and set **Offset** to move the conversation: enter pixels or use **− / +** for 10 px steps, or drag the slider below it. The slider's ends put the conversation against either side of the conversation area, and its notch marks no offset. A dashed line marks the conversation's center while you drag, and briefly after you change the offset or center. In **Settings → Appearance**, **Restore defaults** resets the font sizes, width, and position. Adjustments are temporary unless you choose **Save**, which saves all four as this browser's defaults. **Reset**, changing sessions, or reloading restores your saved appearance settings.
- **Permissions:** Change permission mode directly beside Send, or press Shift+Tab in the message input to cycle through enabled modes. Select the profile beside permissions while the session is idle. Settings contains approval mode, working directory, skills, and delivery options. Images and skills can be sent while the session is idle.
- **Projects:** A project gives the sessions on it memories of their own, beside the global ones. Add, rename, and remove projects on the **Projects** page; meka removes one only once no memory or session is on it. Change a session's project under **Settings** while it is idle; the session list names it. On the **Memories** page, choose a project to see only its memories, and file a new memory in one. Search there is meka's own, the one the agent uses, so it finds words in a memory's body too. A memory stays in the project it was filed in.
- **Approvals:** Pending tool approvals remain visible when you move to another screen. A prompt from a sub-agent names it and links to its session. A prompt answered in another tab, expired, or canceled with its turn closes everywhere.
- **Questions:** An MCP server can ask you something during a tool call: a form to fill in, or a page to open. Its card waits beside the approvals. **Submit** or **Open** answers, **Decline** refuses, and **Skip** leaves it unanswered. Only a web address is opened. Sessions created before mekaweb 0.29.0 decline these without asking.
- **Workspace:** Collapse the navigation or session list to focus on a conversation. Files and the session details, with context, tasks and schedules, stay open until closed, side by side where there is room; where there is not, the one opened last shows. Drag a panel divider to resize it, or use arrow keys when the divider has keyboard focus. Double-click resets its width.
- **Keyboard shortcuts:** Open **Shortcuts** in the navigation, or press **Ctrl+/** (**Cmd+/** on macOS), to see shortcuts for new, delete, rename, previous/next session, stopping a turn, session search, and the terminal. Keyboard deletion always asks for confirmation; previous/next leaves text-field navigation unchanged.
- **Session titles:** Click the conversation heading to rename it. Enter, the checkmark, or clicking away saves; Escape or the cancel button discards the edit. Clearing the title restores the first-message label.
- **Sessions:** Search conversations, sub-agents' included, with the search button beside **New session** or **Ctrl+K** (**Cmd+K** on macOS); **Escape** or the button closes the search. Each session's status icon shows its state with a shape as well as a colour: a green spinner while running, an amber triangle while a tool call waits for approval, and a small grey dot once read. A session with activity since you last viewed it gets a bold title and a blue check if its last turn finished, a red cross if it failed, or a dot when the outcome is unknown, such as for a canceled turn. Opening it clears the mark. Statuses come from the server and update as they change, whichever tab, client, or schedule ran the turn; a session another process runs, such as meka's terminal interface, updates within five minutes. Read state is kept in this browser, so other browsers track their own. Sub-agents show when they are running but are not marked unread, since their results reach the parent. Sub-agents stay hidden until you use the arrow at the edge of their parent's row, which lists them or hides them again, even for the open session. It shows when you point at or focus the row, or while sub-agents are listed beneath it, and always on touch screens. A running sub-agent's page shows its work as it happens. In the conversation, an `agent_spawn` or `agent_followup` call links to its sub-agent and, while the sub-agent runs, lists the calls it makes. Hover over a session or focus its row to reveal Rename, Pin, and Delete. These buttons stay visible in narrow and touch layouts, beside the title when space permits. Pins stay at the top of the list and are saved on the server. Use **Use first message** in Rename to reset a title. The session menu also offers fork, compact, rewind, export, and delete. Import accepts a meka JSON archive. Session-row deletion asks for confirmation; **Shift-click deletes immediately**, including sub-agent sessions.
- **Checklist:** While the agent has items open on its checklist, a strip above the message input shows how many and the one it is working on. Open it for the whole list, with each deferred item's reason. meka keeps a turn going until the pending and in-progress items are done, canceled, or deferred.
- **Notices:** What the agent or a provider reports during a turn appears in that turn, where it happened, until the turn is saved. Warnings and errors also stay below the conversation until the next turn starts.
- **History:** The conversation shows meka's current model context. Compaction and rewind can replace it. While meka compacts, whether asked or on its own, the turn shows **Compacting context…**; then the summary's row says what set it going, such as context usage crossing the ceiling, and opens to the summary. Export the full transcript when you need earlier history. When meka sends the agent back to work, such as to items left open on its checklist, the turn shows a note; expand it to read what meka wrote.
- **Turn summary:** Each turn that has ended closes with how and when it ended and how long it ran, such as "Finished at 10:36 after 9m 36s". Point at it for its details: start and end times, tokens in and out, the share read from cache, and what started it or stopped it early. A click or tap keeps them open.
- **Message actions:** Hover over or focus a message to copy, edit, or delete it, or an agent turn to copy its reply, run it again, or branch from it into a new session. The latest message and turn always show theirs, as does every message on a touch screen. Editing sends the edited message in place of the original, and running a turn again sends its message again; both replace everything after it, as deleting does. When later turns would be lost, mekaweb asks first; Shift-click skips the question. Files the agent changed stay changed. Branching copies the conversation up to that turn and leaves this one as it is. A change is refused when the conversation has changed since it was shown, such as by another tab; check it and try again. A message's images are sent again with it.

New sessions stream thinking when the provider supplies it. Injected context is hidden by default; enable **Show context added by meka** under **Settings → meka** to inspect it.

Withdraw and delivery-recovery controls appear inside their messages. If a submission has an uncertain outcome, inspect saved state before sending again. While it is unknown whether meka received it, **Retry same submission** sends it again under the same key, which meka answers with what the first attempt did. A turn known to have started is not offered again, since meka forgets those keys when it restarts. A queued message whose turn failed in a way waiting can help shows when meka will try it again, and why.

The interface supports Markdown tables, math, diagrams, authenticated image attachments, and code blocks with highlighting for Shiki's bundled languages, copying, and optional line wrapping. Math sits between `$` or `$$`, `\(` and `\)`, or `\[` and `\]`, or stands alone as an environment such as `\begin{align}`; a price such as `$5` stays text, and copying a formula copies its TeX. Collapsed tool calls show their primary argument when available; expand a call to inspect its full arguments and result. See [API support](api-coverage.md) for available operations and backend limitations.

Completion alerts are configured under **Settings → Notifications**, with a **Test** button for each option:

- **Push notifications** come from mekaweb through your browser's push service, so they arrive with mekaweb closed or the phone locked: when a turn finishes or fails, or waits for your approval or answer. Turn them on for each device. A device whose event stream of a conversation is open, such as one showing it, is not notified of it, and a device with mekaweb focused is not notified of turns ending while it shows toasts or plays the chime. Sub-agents' turns and compactions notify nobody. Each notification shows the conversation's title and what happened, never what the agent wrote; tapping it opens the conversation. They need HTTPS, `push.subject` set in mekaweb's config, the browser's permission, and a browser using Google's, Mozilla's, Apple's or Microsoft's push service, which all common browsers do. On iPhone and iPad they need the Home Screen app (see below), where the app's icon also counts the approvals waiting.
- **In-app notifications** are enabled by default. When a different conversation finishes or fails, wherever its turn was started, a toast shows its title and opens it when clicked. Toasts dismiss after six seconds, pausing while hovered or keyboard-focused.
- **Completion sound** is optional and plays a short chime when a turn finishes in any conversation, the one you're viewing included, whether mekaweb is focused or not. A push notification that arrives as well plays the operating system's sound too. Audio may require a click or key press after reloading the app.

In a session created since mekaweb 0.20.0, an approval the turn asks for while nobody is watching waits for you to come back, for up to 30 minutes, rather than being refused.

## Files

The folder button in a session's header opens **Files** in its own panel beside the conversation, left of the session details, at the session's working directory; on the new-session screen it opens at the folder chosen for it. On a phone it opens over the conversation. Files reaches whatever mekaweb's user may read, not only the session's folder: go up with `..` at the top of the list, use a folder in the path, or click the path, or its pencil, to type one, such as `/etc`, `~/notes`, or on Windows `D:\Projects`, and press **Enter** to go there. Closed and opened again, Files is where you left it, for each session until the page reloads; the house button goes to your home folder, and the crosshair button back to the working directory. Open a folder to go into it, and a file to view it, with a double click, **Enter**, or a tap: text and code with line numbers and highlighting, Markdown rendered or as source, pictures, sound, video, and a link to open a PDF. The viewer is sized to the file: text gets most of the window, a picture or video its own size, and sound or a PDF's link a small card. HTML and SVG never run as pages of mekaweb: they download, or show as a picture. The list changes as the folder does, whatever changed it, while the page is in sight, within two seconds on Windows; a disk shared over the network may not tell of changes made from other computers, which **Refresh** shows.

- **Mention in message**, in an entry's menu, its preview, the folder's **…** menu, or a finished upload, adds its path to the message you are writing, so the agent knows which file you mean.
- **Upload** files with the button, or a folder with **Upload folder** in the folder's **…** menu, or drop files and folders on the panel. A folder arrives with the folders inside it, empty ones included. Ten files upload at a time, in 8 MiB pieces, and carry on after a dropped connection, from where mekaweb stands. A file of the same name is replaced only if you say so, for that file or for the whole upload. Files picked or dropped together are listed together, a folder as one line, and dismissed together. Uploads go on while you are elsewhere in the app.
- **Selecting:** click an entry to select it, and double-click it or press **Enter** to open it. Ctrl-click (⌘-click on a Mac) adds to the selection, Shift-click selects a range, Ctrl+A selects everything, and **Escape** or a click beside the list lets go. A right click opens an entry's menu, or, on one of several selected, the selection's. On a touch screen a tap opens an entry and a long press selects it, after which taps select. With more than one selected, the bar above the list mentions them, downloads them as one zip, moves them into a folder, or deletes them, together; those an action fails on stay selected.
- **New folder** makes one in the folder shown; the **…** menu starts a session in it, uploads a folder into it, downloads it as a zip, or copies its path.
- Each entry's menu downloads it (a folder as a zip), renames or moves it to any path, never over something already there, copies its path, or deletes it.
- **Delete** is for good, after asking: there is no trash. Deleting a folder never reaches into a disk mounted inside it: what is on that disk, and the folders holding it, are left. On Windows a file marked read-only is deleted too.
- The eye button shows hidden files: those whose names start with a dot, and on Windows those marked hidden. Folders come first, then files, by name.

A `file_read`, `file_write` or `file_edit` call in the conversation has **Open in Files**, which opens the panel at the file and previews it.

Files acts as mekaweb's user, which can do no more there than its shell can.

## Terminal

The terminal button in a session's header opens the session's terminal, docked under the conversation, and starts your login shell, PowerShell on Windows, or mekaweb's `terminal.shell`, on the meka host, as mekaweb's user, in the session's working directory. **Ctrl+`** opens it too, on macOS as well, and then hides and shows it. Drag its top edge, or focus it and use the arrow keys, to set its height, and double-click it to reset. Its text takes **Terminal font size** from **Settings → Appearance**. **Ctrl** with the mouse wheel or a touchpad pinch over the terminal, or **Ctrl+=**, **Ctrl+-** and **Ctrl+0** in it (**Cmd** on macOS), size its text rather than the page until the terminal is hidden or the page reloads. On a phone it fills the screen.

The terminal belongs to its session. Hiding it leaves a bar under the conversation while its shell runs; select the bar to bring the terminal back. Leaving the session, losing the connection or switching devices leaves it running too: opening it again replays its recent output and carries on. When the shell ends, as on `exit`, the terminal closes. Deleting the session closes it, and one nobody is attached to or typing in closes after mekaweb's `terminal.idle_timeout`, 24 hours unless set. **Close** hangs it up, as closing a terminal window does: its shell and the jobs it started are sent `SIGHUP`, and the shell's process group is killed if it has not ended a few seconds later. On Windows, closing it closes the console its shell runs in, and the shell is ended if it is still running a few seconds later.

On a touch screen a row of keys sits below the terminal: Ctrl (for the next key typed), Esc, Tab, the arrows, `|`, `~`, `/` and `-`.

## meka's status

**Settings → meka** shows whether meka answers, its version and whether this mekaweb supports it, when mekaweb last checked, and meka's health; **Check now** asks again. While meka cannot be reached, mekaweb shows why in place of the app, with **Try again**.

It also names meka's config file and data folder, and when meka last read the file. After editing `config.toml` by hand or with `meka profile` or `meka mcp`, it says the file was edited since; **Reload config** has meka read it again, without a restart, and says which MCP servers it started, restarted, updated or removed. Sections only a restart applies, such as `permissions`, are listed under **Awaiting restart**. **Standing instructions** shows what every session runs under; **Edit** changes them when they come from meka's `instructions.md`, and sessions opened afterwards follow them.

## Providers

**Providers** lists meka's accounts, each with the profiles that run on it beneath. A fresh meka has neither, and mekaweb points here until it does. Click a profile to edit it; an account's **+** adds a profile on it, and each row's **⋯** holds the rest. Drag an account or a profile by its handle, or press the arrow keys on the handle, to reorder them; profile pickers list profiles under their accounts in that order.

- **Accounts:** **New account** adds one for a backend. An API backend takes its key there, and **Change key** replaces it. A Claude or ChatGPT subscription signs in through the browser: **Start sign-in**, open the page, sign in, and paste the code it shows. ChatGPT instead sends the browser to a `localhost` address, which may not load on another machine; paste that address. On meka's own machine the ChatGPT sign-in can finish by itself. Rename moves the account's profiles and sign-in with it; meka refuses to remove an account a profile uses. An account's **Advanced** settings are fixed once it is added.
- **Profiles:** A profile names an account and a model, plus fallbacks to try in order when it can't serve a turn. **Advanced** holds the settings its backend accepts, such as the context window, effort, and thinking; one left blank keeps meka's default. On the OpenAI Responses API, a model that does not reason, such as `gpt-4.1`, needs **Encrypted reasoning** and **Reasoning summary** off. The **Default** profile is the one new sessions use unless they pick another.

## MCP servers

The **MCP** page lists meka's MCP servers, their state, and, for one that failed, why. **Add server** adds a command or an HTTP server. A command runs on meka's machine as meka's user. An HTTP server can present a bearer token, sign in with OAuth, or use client credentials. **Edit** changes a server, restarting it when how it connects changed. meka never shows environment or header values: enter one to set or replace it, or remove a key with its ×; the rest are kept, as is a stored token or secret left blank. Credentials left from a removed server are listed below the servers, to remove. **Sign in** stores an HTTP server's token or secret, or opens an OAuth sign-in; **Sign out** clears what is stored. **View tools** lists a connected server's tools.

## Data saver

**Settings → Data saver** is set on each device; a phone cannot tell mekaweb whether it is on Wi-Fi. With it on, a conversation opens with its last 30 messages rather than 100, its pictures load when you tap them, a command's output is not streamed as it runs (its result shows when it ends), and replies arrive in batches about twice a second.

Whatever it is set to, mekaweb compresses what it sends, reads only the messages that came after the ones shown when a turn ends, follows meka's change feed rather than asking again every few seconds, and sends an edit's changed lines rather than the whole file before and after.

## iPhone and iPad

In Safari, use **Share → Add to Home Screen**. Leave **Open as Web App** enabled if shown. mekaweb opens with the meka icon and its own app window. It still needs a network connection to load and reach meka.

Sign in inside the installed app: its storage is separate from Safari's. Turn on push notifications there; iOS offers them only to the Home Screen app, and asks for permission when you turn them on. iOS suspends the app when you switch away, which closes its live connection; push notifications still arrive, and coming back reconnects. See [WebKit's notes on Home Screen web apps](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/).

## Local data and connection

mekaweb keeps the account, devices, push subscriptions, and the preferences every device shares, **Show context added by meka**, **In-app notifications**, **Completion sound** and the order of **Providers**, in its database on the server. The browser keeps appearance, window layout, Data saver, notification permission, read marks, and text drafts. File attachments are kept in memory and are lost on reload. Clearing browser data removes those, and signs the browser out, but does not delete server sessions.

If mekaweb or meka becomes unreachable, one banner appears above the workspace. mekaweb checks for recovery automatically; **Retry** checks immediately. Recovery preserves your open view and drafts and does not repeat submissions. Warnings about uncertain actions remain with those actions.

mekaweb reaches meka with one token with every scope, so every control is available. A token without `config:r` and `config:w` leaves Providers, MCP changes and the instructions editor read-only. Sessions are meka's own; mekaweb's one user sees them all.
