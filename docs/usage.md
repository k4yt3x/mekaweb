# Using Meka Studio

This guide covers signing in and the interface's behavior in detail. See [Installing Meka Studio](install.md) for setup.

## Signing in

Open Meka Studio at its address and sign in with your username and password, or with **Use a passkey**. Where the browser offers it, the username field suggests your passkeys as you focus it. A sign-in lasts 30 days after its last use, up to a year. A wrong password is refused after as long as a right one takes to check. Repeated tries from one network, or password guesses at the account from everywhere, wait before trying again; a passkey is never held up by someone else guessing the password.

Your name at the foot of the sidebar opens **Settings → Account**, the gear after it opens **Settings**, and the last button signs out. **Account** holds:

- **Passkeys:** **Add a passkey** makes one on this device or a security key, named as you choose. Passkeys need Meka Studio to be reached by name over HTTPS, or on `localhost`; a passkey made at one hostname works only there. Removing one stops it signing in to Meka Studio; the authenticator keeps its copy until you delete it there.
- **Password:** at least 10 characters, and not the username. Changing it takes the current one, and signs out every other device.
- **Devices:** every browser signed in, with its browser and system, when it was last seen, and its address; point at one for when and how it signed in. Rename one, or sign it out; signing a device out ends what it has open, its conversations' live updates and its terminals' connections, and its push notifications. **Sign out other devices** signs out every device but this one.
- **Sign-in activity:** recent sign-ins, confirmations and password changes, refused ones included, with their address and device.

Adding or removing a passkey needs you to have confirmed who you are in the last 10 minutes: Meka Studio asks for a passkey, or the password if you have none. Signing in counts.

Signing out forgets what this browser kept of the account: unsent drafts, which conversations it showed, the files and folders it had open, and uploads under way. If Meka Studio cannot be reached to end the sign-in, the device stays signed in and says so.

## Features

Start a conversation by describing a task and sending it. The folder button below the composer sets the working directory: enter an absolute path on the Meka server, or choose one of your recent directories. Meka refuses a folder that is not there; **Make this folder** makes it, with any folders above it. Permission mode, profile and project sit beside Send; approval mode and delivery options are under **Settings**. A session is created only when you send. **New session** sets this screen up like the session you are in; pressed again, or with no session open, it uses the server's defaults. Either way, an unsent message and its images stay. If the first message fails after creation, retry in that session.

- **Messages:** Enter sends; Shift+Enter adds a newline. The composer starts at two lines and grows with longer drafts, up to three quarters of the conversation's height. Drag its top grip, or focus it and use arrow keys, to set a height yourself; it then stays fixed until the next send. Its height resets after a successful send. When idle, a message starts a turn, which keeps running if you close the tab. While the agent works, messages use Steer by default; choose Queue or Interrupt in the composer's Settings. When a turn is running and the input is empty, Send becomes a red Stop button for that turn. A scheduled job's fire and the report of a finished background task show as notes from Meka, within the agent's turn when a report reaches it mid-turn; expand one to read what the agent was given.
- **Images:** Attach images with the image button, by pasting them into the message input, or by dropping them anywhere on the conversation. A paste that also holds text, as from a spreadsheet, pastes the text. Images are sent as they are up to Meka's 3.75 MB limit, in PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP, or a raster format Meka converts, such as BMP or TIFF. A larger image is shrunk in the browser until it fits, keeping its format where it can (a PNG becomes a JPEG only when it would otherwise shrink below 1024 pixels), and its name says so; a shrunk image loses its metadata, such as where a photo was taken. To give the agent an original, upload it in the session's Files and mention it. Other files, including SVG, HEIC, and PDF, are refused.
- **Conversation appearance:** Set **Conversation font size** and **Conversation max width** under **Settings → Appearance**. Both are kept in this browser, as is the rest of **Appearance**, since screens differ. Width applies to the chat and composer, with a full-width option; font size applies to messages, their contents, and the text you type. Other pages and control text retain their sizes.
- **Conversation position:** The conversation is centered on the page by default, so opening or closing panels does not move it. Set **Center conversation on** to **Conversation area** to center it between the panels instead. **Conversation offset** moves it by a number of pixels, negative to the left. For vertical browser tabs or a tab sidebar, about half the sidebar's width re-centers it on the window. The conversation always stays within the conversation area at its full width; beside the editor or the terminal, or where it cannot be centered on the page without reaching an edge of its area, it is centered in its area instead, without the offset.
- **Reading options:** Open **Aa** in the session or new conversation header to enter a font size or max width in pixels, then press **Enter** to apply. The **− / +** buttons and arrow keys apply 1 px font or 100 px width steps immediately. Leave width blank for full width; width also applies to the composer. Under **Position**, choose **Page** or **Area** to center on, and set **Offset** to move the conversation: enter pixels or use **− / +** for 10 px steps, or drag the slider below it. The slider's ends put the conversation against either side of the conversation area, and its notch marks no offset. A dashed line marks the conversation's center while you drag, and briefly after you change the offset or center. In **Settings → Appearance**, **Restore defaults** resets the font sizes, width, and position. Adjustments are temporary unless you choose **Save**, which saves all four as this browser's defaults. **Reset**, changing sessions, or reloading restores your saved appearance settings.
- **Permissions:** Change permission mode directly beside Send, or press Shift+Tab in the message input to cycle through enabled modes. Select the profile beside permissions while the session is idle. Settings contains approval mode, working directory, skills, and delivery options. Images and skills can be sent while the session is idle.
- **Projects:** A project gives the sessions on it memories of their own, beside the global ones. Add, rename, and remove projects on the **Projects** page; Meka removes one only once no memory or session is on it. The project beside the profile is the one the session is on; choose another while the session is idle, or **New project…** to add one and put the session on it. It shows once a project exists, or where you may add one. Pointing at the session's row in the sidebar shows the project too, with its profile. On the **Memories** page, choose a project to see only its memories, and file a new memory in one. Search there is Meka's own, the one the agent uses, so it finds words in a memory's body too. A memory stays in the project it was filed in.
- **Approvals:** Pending tool approvals remain visible when you move to another screen. A prompt from a sub-agent names it and links to its session. A prompt answered in another tab, expired, or canceled with its turn closes everywhere.
- **Questions:** An MCP server can ask you something during a tool call: a form to fill in, or a page to open. Its card waits beside the approvals. **Submit** or **Open** answers, **Decline** refuses, and **Skip** leaves it unanswered. Only a web address is opened. Sessions created before Meka Studio 0.29.0 decline these without asking.
- **Sidebar:** The sidebar at the left leads to **Projects**, **Memories**, **Skills** and **Schedules**, which open beside it, then lists your sessions, with search and **New session** beside their heading. At its foot are your name, **Import archive**, **Settings** and **Sign out**. Collapse it to a column of those icons to focus on a conversation. Pinned sessions come first, each marked by a pin, with a line between them and the rest. Once a session is on a project, the rest are grouped: each project's by name with how many it holds, then **No project**. A group shows its newest, and more as its end comes into view. Click a group's heading to fold it, which this browser remembers; folded, its heading shows a status icon when one of its recent sessions is running, waiting for approval, or unread. The **+** on a project's heading, or on **No project**, starts a new session on it. Search lists matches from every project, each naming its project beneath its title.
- **Settings** opens over the session you are in, from the gear at the foot of the sidebar. Its sections are **General**, with what a conversation shows and **Data saver**; **Account**; **Appearance**; **Notifications**; Meka's **Providers**, **MCP** servers and standing **Instructions**; **Keyboard shortcuts**; and **About**, with what is running. Escape closes it, except in a field you are typing in. The addresses these had as pages, such as `#/providers`, open their section.
- **Workspace:** The buttons down the right edge open one panel at a time beside the conversation: **Files**, **Context**, **Tasks** and **Schedules**, with **Ctrl+Shift+1** to **4** (Control on macOS too). A panel stays open until its button is pressed again, and shows again where it was left. **Tasks** counts the background tasks running. The terminal's button sits below them. A file opened from **Files**, or a terminal, shows in the editor, between the conversation and the panel; where both do not fit, the one opened last shows. Drag a divider to resize what is beside it, or use arrow keys when the divider has keyboard focus. Double-click resets its width. Where the sidebar does not fit beside what is open, it collapses to its icons; showing it again closes the panel if that leaves it no room, and narrows the rest. On a phone the header has **Files**, the terminal, and **Session details**, which holds the other three.
- **Context:** Beside **Send**, a ring shows how full the session's context is, amber within ten points of where Meka compacts it. Point at it for the numbers; click it to open **Context**.
- **Keyboard shortcuts:** Open **Settings → Keyboard shortcuts**, or press **Ctrl+/** (**Cmd+/** on macOS), which shows and hides them, to see shortcuts for new, delete, rename, previous/next session, stopping a turn, session search, the terminal, and the panels. Keyboard deletion always asks for confirmation; previous/next leaves text-field navigation unchanged.
- **Session titles:** Click the conversation heading to rename it. Enter, the checkmark, or clicking away saves; Escape or the cancel button discards the edit. Clearing the title restores the first-message label.
- **Sessions:** Search conversations, sub-agents' included, with the search button beside **Sessions** in the sidebar or **Ctrl+K** (**Cmd+K** on macOS); **Escape** or the button closes the search. Each session takes one row: its title and the day it last changed. Its status icon shows its state with a shape as well as a colour: a green spinner while running, an amber triangle while a tool call waits for approval, and a small grey dot once read. A session with activity since you last viewed it gets a bold title and a blue check if its last turn finished, a red cross if it failed, or a dot when the outcome is unknown, such as for a canceled turn. Opening it clears the mark. Statuses come from the server and update as they change, whichever tab, client, or schedule ran the turn; a session another process runs, such as Meka's terminal interface, updates within five minutes. Read state is kept in this browser, so other browsers track their own. Sub-agents show when they are running but are not marked unread, since their results reach the parent. Sub-agents stay hidden until you use the arrow on their parent's row, which lists them or hides them again, even for the open session. It takes the place of the status icon when you point at the row or reach it with the keyboard; on touch screens it stays at the row's edge. A running sub-agent's page shows its work as it happens. In the conversation, an `agent_spawn` or `agent_followup` call links to its sub-agent and, while the sub-agent runs, lists the calls it makes. Hover over a session or focus its row to reveal Rename, Pin, and Delete. These buttons stay visible in narrow and touch layouts, beside the title when space permits. Pins stay at the top of the list and are saved on the server. Use **Use first message** in Rename to reset a title. The session menu also offers fork, compact, rewind, export as a JSON archive, and delete. Import accepts a Meka JSON archive. Session-row deletion asks for confirmation; **Shift-click deletes immediately**, including sub-agent sessions. A session with files open in its editor that have changes not saved is not deleted until they are saved or closed.
- **Checklist:** While the agent has items open on its checklist, a strip above the message input shows how many and the one it is working on. Open it for the whole list, with each deferred item's reason. Meka keeps a turn going until the pending and in-progress items are done, canceled, or deferred.
- **Notices:** What the agent or a provider reports during a turn appears in that turn, where it happened, until the turn is saved. Warnings and errors also stay below the conversation, as does a notice that comes between turns, until the next turn starts or you dismiss one with its **×**; one said again shows there once, where it was said last. A provider's failure names what the provider answered, and text past three lines folds, with **Show more**.
- **History:** The conversation shows everything said, the turns a compaction summarized included; earlier messages load as you scroll up. A rewind takes its turns out. When Meka compacts, whether asked or on its own, a line across the conversation marks where; while it works, the line reads **Compacting context…** and light runs along it toward the middle. Open it for what set it going, such as context usage crossing the ceiling, and for the summary the agent works from. **Compact context** in the session menu leaves the agent the summary alone; turn on **Keep recent turns** to keep the latest turns after it as they were. When Meka sends the agent back to work, such as to items left open on its checklist, the turn shows a note; expand it to read what Meka wrote.
- **Turn summary:** Each turn that has ended closes with how and when it ended and how long it ran, such as "Finished at 10:36 after 9m 36s". Point at it for its details: start and end times, tokens in and out, the share read from cache, and what started it or stopped it early. A click or tap keeps them open.
- **Message actions:** Hover over or focus a message to copy, edit, or delete it, or an agent turn to copy its reply, run it again, or branch from it into a new session. The latest message and turn always show theirs, as does every message on a touch screen. Editing sends the edited message in place of the original, and running a turn again sends its message again; both replace everything after it, as deleting does. When later turns would be lost, Meka Studio asks first; Shift-click skips the question. Files the agent changed stay changed. Branching copies the conversation up to that turn and leaves this one as it is. Only turns the agent still has in context offer these: those since the last compaction and those it kept. A change is refused when the conversation has changed since it was shown, such as by another tab; check it and try again. A message's images are sent again with it.

New sessions stream thinking when the provider supplies it. Injected context is hidden by default; enable **Show context added by Meka** under **Settings → General** to inspect it.

The agent's thinking and tool calls show as they happen. Once it writes more after them, or its turn ends, each run of them folds into one line that says what it did, such as "Ran 5 commands, read 2 files, thought 3 times", in red if a call failed; select it to open the run. A run that finishes while you are scrolled up stays open until you are back at the latest. Changes to files, memories and skills never fold, and show what they change: an edit's lines taken out and put in, and a write's new text, a dozen lines at first, with **Show all** for the rest. Turn off **Fold finished steps** under **Settings → General** to keep every step open. Turn on **Expand thinking as it streams** there to read the agent's thinking as it comes: it shows open while it streams and folds once the agent moves on, or, if you scrolled up, once you are back at the latest. When the model returns nothing, the conversation says so. A tool's output too large to keep shows its opening, and names the scratchpad entry that holds all of it.

Withdraw and delivery-recovery controls appear inside their messages. If a submission has an uncertain outcome, inspect saved state before sending again. While it is unknown whether Meka received it, **Retry same submission** sends it again under the same key, which Meka answers with what the first attempt did. A turn known to have started is not offered again, since Meka forgets those keys when it restarts. A queued message whose turn failed in a way waiting can help shows when Meka will try it again, and why.

The interface supports Markdown tables, math, diagrams, authenticated image attachments, and code blocks with highlighting for Shiki's bundled languages, copying, and optional line wrapping. Math sits between `$` or `$$`, `\(` and `\)`, or `\[` and `\]`, or stands alone as an environment such as `\begin{align}`; a price such as `$5` stays text, and copying a formula copies its TeX. Collapsed tool calls show their primary argument when available; expand a call to inspect its full arguments and result. See [API support](api-coverage.md) for available operations and backend limitations.

Completion alerts are configured under **Settings → Notifications**, with a **Test** button for each option:

- **Push notifications** come from Meka Studio through your browser's push service, so they arrive with Meka Studio closed or the phone locked: when a turn finishes or fails, or waits for your approval or answer. Turn them on for each device. A device whose event stream of a conversation is open, such as one showing it, is not notified of it, and a device with Meka Studio focused is not notified of turns ending while it shows toasts or plays the chime. Sub-agents' turns and compactions notify nobody. Each notification shows the conversation's title and what happened, never what the agent wrote; tapping it opens the conversation. They need HTTPS, `push.subject` set in Meka Studio's config, the browser's permission, and a browser using Google's, Mozilla's, Apple's or Microsoft's push service, which all common browsers do. On iPhone and iPad they need the Home Screen app (see below), where the app's icon also counts the approvals waiting.
- **In-app notifications** are enabled by default. When a different conversation finishes or fails, wherever its turn was started, a toast shows its title and opens it when clicked. Toasts dismiss after six seconds, pausing while hovered or keyboard-focused.
- **Completion sound** is optional and plays a short chime when a turn finishes in any conversation, the one you're viewing included, whether Meka Studio is focused or not. A push notification that arrives as well plays the operating system's sound too. Audio may require a click or key press after reloading the app.

In a session created since Meka Studio 0.20.0, an approval the turn asks for while nobody is watching waits for you to come back, for up to 30 minutes, rather than being refused.

## Files

The folder button at the right edge of a session opens **Files** in the side panel, at the session's working directory; on the new-session screen it opens at the folder chosen for it. On a phone it is in the header, and opens over the conversation. Files shows a folder as a tree, with an icon for each kind of file and folder, as VS Code's Material Icon Theme draws them. It reaches whatever Meka Studio's user may read, not only the session's folder: click a folder in the path above the tree, or choose **Show from here** in a folder's menu, to show the tree from there, or click the path, or its pencil, to type one, such as `/etc`, `~/notes`, or on Windows `D:\Projects`, and press **Enter**. The path is at the top, and under it the buttons for the folder and, if it is in a git repository, its branch; the **…** menu goes to your home folder or back to the working directory. Closed and opened again, or after a reload, Files is where you left it, its folders open as they were, for each session, as this browser remembers. The tree changes as the folders it shows do, whatever changed them, while the page is in sight, within two seconds on Windows; a disk shared over the network may not tell of changes made from other computers, which **Refresh**, in the **…** menu, shows. The tree's text takes **Files font size** from **Settings → Appearance**. **Ctrl** with the mouse wheel or a touchpad pinch over the tree, or **Ctrl+=**, **Ctrl+-** and **Ctrl+0** in it or with the pointer over it (**Cmd** on macOS), size its text rather than the page until Files closes.

- **Opening:** click a folder, or its arrow, to open or close it, and double-click a file to open it in the editor (see below). On a touch screen a tap opens either. **Collapse folders** closes them all.
- **Keyboard:** the arrow keys move through the tree, **→** and **←** opening and closing folders; **Home**, **End** and typing a name jump. **Enter** opens, **Space** selects, **F2** renames, and **Delete** deletes, after asking.
- **Selecting:** Ctrl-click (⌘-click on a Mac) adds to the selection, Shift-click selects a range, even across folders, Ctrl+A selects everything shown, and **Escape** or a click beside the entries lets go. On a touch screen a long press selects, after which taps select. A right click, or an entry's **…** button, opens the menu for what is selected. With more than one selected, the bar that takes the buttons' place mentions them, downloads them as one zip, moves them into a folder, or deletes them, together; those an action fails on stay selected.
- **New file** and **New folder** add a row to name one, in the folder selected or the one holding the file selected, else at the top. A new file opens in the editor.
- **Rename** names an entry again in its row; **Move to…** moves it into any folder. Neither replaces what is there. Drag entries onto a folder to move them there.
- **Mention in message**, in the menu, the folder's **…** menu, a tab's menu, or a finished upload, adds the path to the message you are writing, so the agent knows which file you mean.
- **Upload** files with the button, or a folder with **Upload folder** in the folder's **…** menu, into the folder selected; or drop files and folders from your computer onto a folder in the tree. A folder arrives with the folders inside it, empty ones included. Ten files upload at a time, in 8 MiB pieces, and carry on after a dropped connection, from where Meka Studio stands. A file of the same name is replaced only if you say so, for that file or for the whole upload. Files picked or dropped together are listed together, a folder as one line, and dismissed together. Uploads go on while you are elsewhere in the app.
- The folder's **…** menu also starts a session or a terminal in it, downloads it as a zip, or copies its path; an entry's starts a terminal in it, or in the folder holding a file, downloads it, a folder as a zip, or copies its path.
- **Delete** is for good, after asking: there is no trash. Deleting a folder never reaches into a disk mounted inside it: what is on that disk, and the folders holding it, are left. On Windows a file marked read-only is deleted too.
- **Show hidden files**, in the **…** menu, shows those whose names start with a dot, and on Windows those marked hidden. Folders come first, then files, by name, numbers by their value.
- **Git:** in a git repository each entry is marked as `git status` says, in the colour of its change: **M** modified, **A** added, **R** renamed, **U** untracked, **!** in conflict. A folder holding changes, or one a file was moved or deleted from, gets a dot, and what git ignores is dimmed. Hover over an entry for what its mark means. The branch shows under the path, with **↑** and **↓** for the commits it is ahead of its upstream and behind; select it for its upstream and commit. The marks follow changes in the folders shown, staging, commits and switching branches, and each tool call the agent finishes. A submodule is marked when its commit changes; inside it, its own changes are marked. Past 10,000 changes none are, and a warning beside the branch says so. Files uses the host's git, 2.31 or newer; without it, nothing is marked.

A `file_read`, `file_write` or `file_edit` call in the conversation has **Open in Files**, which shows the file in the tree and opens it in the editor.

Files acts as Meka Studio's user, which can do no more there than its shell can. Showing a repository runs no program its own git config names, such as a filter, so one unpacked from an archive cannot run anything by being shown; those in your own git config, such as Git LFS's, still run.

## Editor

A file opened from Files shows in the editor, between the conversation and the side panel, a tab for each file and terminal open; drag a tab to move it, middle-click it, or its **×**, to close it, and right-click it for more. The **+** after the tabs makes a **New file…**, empty, at the path you give, offering the folder of the tab shown, or starts a **New terminal**. Text and code open to edit, in Monaco, the editor of VS Code, with its keys: Ctrl+F (⌘F) finds, Ctrl+H replaces, Alt-click adds cursors, and F1 or Ctrl+Shift+P lists every command. **Alt+Z** (⌥Z), or the wrap button beside save, wraps long lines, as this browser remembers. The line below shows where the cursor is, how the file indents, its line endings and its language. Its text takes **Editor font size** from **Settings → Appearance**. **Ctrl** with the mouse wheel or a touchpad pinch over it, or **Ctrl+=**, **Ctrl+-** and **Ctrl+0** in it (**Cmd** on macOS), size its text rather than the page until the editor closes or the page reloads. On a touch screen they open in CodeMirror instead, which selects and types as the system does. Code is highlighted with the conversation's colours. Pictures, sound and video show as they are; a PDF opens in a tab of its own, and anything else can be downloaded. HTML and SVG never run as pages of Meka Studio. **Preview** shows Markdown rendered. On a phone the editor covers the screen, its tabs in the menu at its top; **×** hides it, leaving them open. This browser remembers each session's tabs, the one shown, and where in each file the cursor and scroll were, so a reload brings them back; a file is read again as you show it, and a terminal comes back while its shell runs. Changes not saved are not kept, so leaving asks first.

- **Saving:** Ctrl+S (⌘S), or the save button above the file, writes the file whole, keeping its mode, its line endings and any byte order mark; a tab's dot shows changes not yet saved. Closing a tab with changes, or leaving the page, asks first; signing out waits until they are saved or closed. A file over 10 MiB opens its first 10 MiB, to read only.
- **Changes on disk:** the editor follows the files it has open. One changed on disk, as by the agent, while it has no changes here, shows what is there now. One with changes here shows a bar instead: **Compare** shows what is on disk beside yours, **Reload** takes what is on disk, and **Keep mine** lets the next save write over it. A save never writes over a file changed since it was opened without asking so. A file deleted on disk stays open, and saving makes it again.
- **Renaming or moving** an open file in Files, or a folder holding it, takes its tab along, changes and all; renamed elsewhere, as with `mv`, it shows as deleted.
- Each session keeps its own tabs, and a file open in two sessions is one file, with one set of changes, until the page reloads.

## Terminal

The terminal button, below the panels' at the right edge of a session, or **Ctrl+`** (on macOS as well), shows the session's terminal in a tab of the editor, starting one if there is none: your login shell, PowerShell on Windows, or Meka Studio's `terminal.shell`, on the Meka host, as Meka Studio's user, in the session's working directory. Pressed again, it goes back to the file shown last. **New terminal** in the **+** menu after the tabs, or **Ctrl+Shift+`**, starts another, and **Open terminal here** in Files starts one in a folder; with several, they are numbered as they started. Their text takes **Terminal font size** from **Settings → Appearance**. **Ctrl** with the mouse wheel or a touchpad pinch over a terminal, or **Ctrl+=**, **Ctrl+-** and **Ctrl+0** in it (**Cmd** on macOS), size their text rather than the page until the editor closes or the page reloads. On a phone the terminal button is in the header, and the terminal covers the screen as the editor does.

Terminals belong to their session, each with a tab for as long as its shell runs. While one runs out of sight, a dot marks the terminal button. Leaving the session, reloading, losing the connection or switching devices leaves them running: their tabs come back, replaying their recent output, and carry on. When a shell ends, as on `exit`, its tab closes. Closing its tab hangs it up, as closing a terminal window does: its shell and the jobs it started are sent `SIGHUP`, and the shell's process group is killed if it has not ended a few seconds later. On Windows, closing it closes the console its shell runs in, and the shell is ended if it is still running a few seconds later. Deleting the session closes its terminals, and one nobody is attached to or typing in closes after Meka Studio's `terminal.idle_timeout`, 24 hours unless set.

On a touch screen a row of keys sits below the terminal: Ctrl (for the next key typed), Esc, Tab, the arrows, `|`, `~`, `/` and `-`.

## Meka's status

**Settings → About** shows whether Meka answers, its version and whether this Meka Studio supports it, when Meka Studio last checked, and Meka's health; **Check now** asks again. While Meka cannot be reached, Meka Studio shows why in place of the app, with **Try again**.

It also names Meka's config file and data folder, and when Meka last read the file. After editing `config.toml` by hand or with `meka profile` or `meka mcp`, it says the file was edited since; **Reload config** has Meka read it again, without a restart, and says which MCP servers it started, restarted, updated or removed. Sections only a restart applies, such as `permissions`, are listed under **Awaiting restart**. **Standing instructions** shows what every session runs under; **Edit** changes them when they come from Meka's `instructions.md`, and sessions opened afterwards follow them.

## Providers

**Settings → Providers** lists Meka's accounts, each with the profiles that run on it beneath. A fresh Meka has neither, and Meka Studio points here until it does. Click a profile to edit it; an account's **+** adds a profile on it, and each row's **⋯** holds the rest. Drag an account or a profile by its handle, or press the arrow keys on the handle, to reorder them; profile pickers list profiles under their accounts in that order.

- **Accounts:** **New account** adds one for a backend. An API backend takes its key there, and **Change key** replaces it. A Claude or ChatGPT subscription signs in through the browser: **Start sign-in**, open the page, sign in, and paste the code it shows. ChatGPT instead sends the browser to a `127.0.0.1` address, which may not load on another machine; paste that address. On Meka's own machine the ChatGPT sign-in can finish by itself. Rename moves the account's profiles and sign-in with it; Meka refuses to remove an account a profile uses. An account's **Advanced** settings are fixed once it is added.
- **Profiles:** A profile names an account and a model, plus fallbacks to try in order when it can't serve a turn. **Advanced** holds the settings its backend accepts, such as the context window, effort, and thinking; one left blank keeps Meka's default. **Request parameters** are fields sent with every request as written, as a JSON object such as `{"temperature": 0.6}`; Meka refuses one it writes itself, and the subscriptions take none. A ChatGPT subscription's profile can ask for a Daybreak **Cyber access program**, such as `daybreak_blue`. On the OpenAI Responses API, a model that does not reason, such as `gpt-4.1`, needs **Encrypted reasoning** and **Reasoning summary** off. The **Default** profile is the one new sessions use unless they pick another.

## MCP servers

**Settings → MCP** lists Meka's MCP servers, their state, and, for one that failed, why. **Add server** adds a command or an HTTP server. A command runs on Meka's machine as Meka's user. An HTTP server can present a bearer token, sign in with OAuth, or use client credentials. **Edit** changes a server, restarting it when how it connects changed. Meka never shows environment or header values: enter one to set or replace it, or remove a key with its ×; the rest are kept, as is a stored token or secret left blank. Credentials left from a removed server are listed below the servers, to remove. **Sign in** stores an HTTP server's token or secret, or opens an OAuth sign-in; **Sign out** clears what is stored. **View tools** lists a connected server's tools.

## Data saver

**Data saver**, in **Settings → General**, is set on each device; a phone cannot tell Meka Studio whether it is on Wi-Fi. With it on, a conversation opens with its last 30 messages rather than 100, its pictures load when you tap them, a command's output is not streamed as it runs (its result shows when it ends), and replies arrive in batches about twice a second.

Whatever it is set to, Meka Studio compresses what it sends, reads only the messages that came after the ones shown when a turn ends, follows Meka's change feed rather than asking again every few seconds, and sends an edit's changed lines rather than the whole file before and after.

## iPhone and iPad

In Safari, use **Share → Add to Home Screen**. Leave **Open as Web App** enabled if shown. Meka Studio opens with the Meka icon and its own app window. It still needs a network connection to load and reach Meka.

Sign in inside the installed app: its storage is separate from Safari's. Turn on push notifications there; iOS offers them only to the Home Screen app, and asks for permission when you turn them on. iOS suspends the app when you switch away, which closes its live connection; push notifications still arrive, and coming back reconnects. See [WebKit's notes on Home Screen web apps](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/).

## Local data and connection

Meka Studio keeps the account, devices, push subscriptions, and the preferences every device shares, **Show context added by Meka**, **Fold finished steps**, **Expand thinking as it streams**, **In-app notifications**, **Completion sound** and the order of **Providers**, in its database on the server. The browser keeps appearance, window layout, the files and folders each session had open, Data saver, notification permission, read marks, and text drafts. File attachments are kept in memory and are lost on reload. Clearing browser data removes those, and signs the browser out, but does not delete server sessions.

If Meka Studio or Meka becomes unreachable, one banner appears above the workspace. Meka Studio checks for recovery automatically; **Retry** checks immediately. Recovery preserves your open view and drafts and does not repeat submissions. Warnings about uncertain actions remain with those actions.

Meka Studio reaches Meka with one token with every scope, so every control is available. A token without `config:r` and `config:w` leaves Providers, MCP changes and the instructions editor read-only. Sessions are Meka's own; Meka Studio's one user sees them all.
